Experiment

This is essentially a **menu of collaboration-sized technical
projects**, ranging from things already producing research results to
raw ideas that could become a weekend experiment. The common theme is
that most are deliberately **small enough to understand and
interrogate**, rather than "throw a giant model at a dataset."

The strongest through-lines are:

-   **Interpretable ML / mathematical experiments.** *Latent Forge*
    studies recommender systems from means and low-rank approximations
    upward; *Pocket Cocktail Party* makes ICA/blind-source separation
    geometrically understandable; *JabberVec* examines the geometry of
    learned character embeddings; *DIAL* scales a tiny learnable
    digital-sundial problem from 3 bits upward.
-   **NLP and multilingual language structure.** *Bilingual Boundary
    Lab* studies Russian/French switching inside *War and Peace*;
    *English Gorf* looks for Germanic/French historical signals in
    English; *MorphoGender* asks how predictable grammatical gender is
    from noun form; proposed projects investigate translated-author
    attribution, multilingual name corruption, ambiguity, and Cyrillic
    OCR errors.
-   **Computer vision + historical documents.** *RMFB/RMMFB* is the
    substantial research lineage: detecting manuscript fragments reused
    in historical bindings, with later work involving high-resolution
    models, explainability and experiment design. *Binding Unwinding*
    handles the decidedly unglamorous but important
    image-extraction/data-engineering side. *Nyquist Text Existence
    Criterion* asks a particularly interesting foundational question:
    **when does text physically cease to exist in a downsampled image,
    independently of whether OCR recognizes it?**
-   **Small, conventional ML projects with room for serious analysis.**
    *Envelope ZIP Reading* offers MNIST neural-net/CNN work with
    emphasis on examining individual failures. The TensorFlow, PyTorch,
    AWS and other coursework repositories are explicitly treated as
    **sources of concepts to rebuild independently**, rather than code
    to recycle.
-   **Research infrastructure and engineering.** *FHTW Paper Code Prep*,
    *Multimodal Jupy Logger*, *Text Cleanroom*, the utility
    repositories, and the Jupyter UI toolkit cover reproducibility,
    provenance, data cleaning, experiment infrastructure, packaging and
    practical software engineering.
-   **More speculative research seeds.** These include model collapse
    through repeated generated-data training, quantitatively measuring
    inconsistent instructions, incomplete ontologies in RLHF, symbolic
    encodings of visual structure, quantization/information persistence,
    computational mechanics, and inverse optics.
-   **Dataset-building projects.** The worldwide *Underground Systems
    Prediction Dataset*, historical-name corruption, ambiguity corpus,
    and OCR-pair collection are opportunities where constructing a
    genuinely interesting dataset is itself much of the intellectual
    work.

There is also a useful **maturity spectrum**. Some projects---especially
RMFB/RMMFB---represent a real research lineage. Latent Forge, NTEC,
Bilingual Boundary Lab, Pocket Cocktail Party, JabberVec and several
tooling projects are active experiments/prototypes. Others are
deliberately **collaboration-ready seeds**, where someone could help
define and run the first clean experiment.

So if I compressed the whole catalog to one sentence:

> **It is a portfolio of small, inspectable ML/data/scientific-computing
> experiments---especially multilingual NLP, interpretable mathematical
> ML, historical-document CV, and research tooling---designed so a
> collaborator can contribute something meaningful without first
> inheriting an enormous project.**

And an important feature of the catalog is that **"upskilling" doesn't
mean doing generic tutorial projects**. Even relatively elementary
techniques---frequency vectors, PCA/SVD, MNIST CNNs, regression, matrix
factorization---are attached to unusual questions or datasets, so
someone can learn the technique while producing something that has a
reason to exist.
