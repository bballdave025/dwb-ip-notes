# IP Classification Framework

This document classifies projects in this repository to clarify intent,
reduce ambiguity, and support responsible intellectual-property handling
across employment contexts.

The goal is not to create artificial boundaries around ideas, but to:

- document provenance,
- clarify development context,
- distinguish independent work from employer-specific implementations,
- and reduce ambiguity regarding ownership, reuse, and scope.

Projects are grouped into five categories.

---

## A. Independent Research

Projects developed independently and intended for academic publication,
open research, or long-term theoretical exploration.

### Characteristics

- Core ideas developed prior to or independently of employment
- Work conducted on personal time
- No use of employer systems, datasets, or confidential information
- Based on public data, personal data, or independently created materials
- Intended for publication, collaboration, or open dissemination

### Examples

- theoretical ML concepts
- manuscript studies
- evaluation frameworks
- exploratory scientific or mathematical models

### Purpose

To preserve the ability to conduct and publish independent research
without ambiguity regarding ownership or scope.

---

## B. Personal ML Learning Experiments

Projects created as part of personal study, skill-building, interview
preparation, or exploration of machine-learning concepts.

### Characteristics

- Motivated by general learning, curiosity, or technical development
- May be inspired by publicly known concepts or general educational exposure
- Not derived from proprietary systems or internal implementations
- Typically smaller in scope and not necessarily intended for publication

### Examples

- toy ML models
- benchmark reproductions
- exploratory notebooks
- architecture experiments
- evaluation exercises

### Purpose

To document technical growth and experimentation without asserting
research ownership or publication intent.

---

## C. Shareable Experiments

Projects that may be of practical value in applied settings and could
potentially be shared voluntarily with collaborators or employers.

### Characteristics

- Exploratory tools, workflows, or ideas
- Independently implemented
- Not tied to proprietary data or systems
- Not intended as exclusive personal research
- Practical rather than primarily theoretical

### Examples

- workflow prototypes
- evaluation helpers
- utility scripts
- exploratory interfaces
- generalized proof-of-concept tooling

### Purpose

To enable collaboration and practical use without creating unnecessary
IP ambiguity.

---

## D. Personal General-Purpose Tooling

Projects developed independently as reusable utilities, workflow tools,
developer infrastructure, or operational support systems intended for
broad applicability across domains and environments.

### Characteristics

- Portable across projects and organizations
- Developed independently and maintained personally
- Focused on reproducibility, observability, workflow support,
  normalization, automation, or developer ergonomics
- Not dependent on proprietary systems, datasets, or confidential logic
- Often used personally across many unrelated projects

### Examples

- notebook tooling
- logging frameworks
- reproducibility helpers
- text normalization utilities
- workflow orchestration tools
- local development infrastructure
- general-purpose data-cleaning utilities

### Purpose

To distinguish independently developed engineering infrastructure from
organization-specific implementations or internal pipelines.

These projects may sometimes resemble tooling used professionally because
many engineering problems are structurally common across domains.
Similarity of problem class does not imply derivation from employer systems.

---

## E. Potentially Integrable Independent Tooling

Projects developed independently but intentionally structured such that
they could later be adapted, integrated, or voluntarily shared within
professional or collaborative environments.

### Characteristics

- Independently designed and repository-scoped personally
- Generalized rather than organization-specific
- Designed for portability and adaptation
- Maintained outside employer systems
- May later inform internal tooling or workflows
- Does not contain proprietary systems, confidential workflows,
  restricted datasets, or internal implementations

### Examples

- notebook execution/logging frameworks
- generalized multimodal tooling
- portable automation systems
- reusable evaluation infrastructure
- configurable workflow frameworks

### Purpose

To clarify that portability and future applicability do not imply
derivation from employer systems or transfer of ownership.

This category is especially relevant for independently maintained tooling
that may later prove useful in professional settings while remaining
conceptually and operationally separate from employer-confidential systems.

---

## General Principles

Across all categories:

- No proprietary datasets, credentials, or confidential system details
  are included.
- Personal repositories are maintained separately from employer systems.
- Similarity to common industry tooling patterns does not imply copying.
- Public ideas, standard engineering practices, and independently developed
  abstractions remain broadly reusable.
- Some projects may evolve between categories over time.

Classification is based primarily on:

- origin of ideas,
- development context,
- resource usage,
- intended use,
- and relationship to employer systems.

---

## Notes on Professional Context

Some independently developed tools may occasionally be useful in
professional environments or inspire future workplace implementations.

However, unless explicitly transferred or developed as part of assigned
work using employer resources, these repositories are intended to remain:

- independently maintained,
- personally scoped,
- and separate from employer-confidential systems or implementations.

Professional exposure may reveal that a general tooling pattern is useful,
but usefulness in a professional context does not by itself make
independently developed abstractions employer-specific.

Employer-specific implementations, datasets, workflows, guidelines,
task details, and confidential systems are intentionally excluded from
these repositories.

This framework exists to support clarity, good-faith separation of
concerns, and responsible IP hygiene.

It is not intended to create artificial exclusivity around broadly
applicable engineering concepts or public-domain techniques.
